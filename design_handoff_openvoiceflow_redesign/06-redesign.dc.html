THE BRIEF, IN ONE LINE
Make the first sixty seconds feel like magic, and the five-hundredth dictation feel like nothing at all.

Phases 01–05 built a system that is technically complete and emotionally flat. Nothing here throws that away. The palette, the ember, the waveform-ring, the spring set — all locked. What changes is sequence, scale, and honesty: what the app says first, how big it says it, and whether it tells you the truth while you wait.

Three moves carry the whole redesign. One line that never breaks — the HUD becomes a single morphing stroke instead of five drawings. Dead time becomes useful time — the model downloads behind the Know-Me interview. The first sentence is kept forever — proof of magic, stored and shown.

01 · CRITIQUE
The system is right. The sequencing is wrong.

Below, the current build recreated from the Swift — HUDController, OnboardingView, DashboardView — at 1:1, with the problems marked in place.

Working — keep

One motif, everywhere. The same win(u) taper and S(t) envelope drive the menu-bar glyph, the HUD, and the empty states. Very few apps have a mathematical signature. This one does.

Ember on warm neutral. #C97B35 over #1D1B18 is not a default. It reads as filament, not as a brand colour, which is exactly right for something that listens in the dark.

Stats already refuse engagement. "Time saved", not "sessions". The instinct is correct — it is just buried in a grid of four equal boxes.

Generic — replace

The dashboard is a SaaS admin panel. Sidebar, four equal stat cards, a bar chart. Four equal cards means no card is the point. Nothing on Home is bigger than 26px, so nothing is the answer to "was this worth installing?"

Dot pagination in onboarding. A web carousel pattern in a Mac window. Mac onboarding names its steps or shows nothing.

Dark-only onboarding. .preferredColorScheme(.dark) — the first thing a light-mode user sees is the app overriding their system setting. It contradicts the "native, calm" premise in the first frame.

Fighting the user — fix

Three Allow buttons at once. Three parallel choices, no stated order, three OS dialogs stacking. The escape hatch ("Not listed? Click +…") appears only after a grant fails — the moment anxiety is already highest.

A progress bar with no number. "Getting ready" could mean ten seconds or eight minutes. A bar without a size, a rate, or an ETA is not honest progress; it is a screensaver.

The proof happens in the wrong place. "Try it" echoes the transcript into a box inside the onboarding window — but the promise is "in any app". The one demonstration is staged in the one place that isn't real, and the payoff is a single line of 12.5px green text.

02 · FIRST RUN, END TO END
Eight frames from download to "it worked". Click through them.

The current flow starts at frame 4 and hopes the user survived the first three alone. Two of these frames are surfaces we already own but never designed — the download page and the DMG. One is owned by macOS and can only be predicted. The rest is the app.

Frames 0–1 · Killing the void
The download page and the DMG background are the only two surfaces that exist between "I want this" and "why is nothing happening". Both currently say nothing. Now they say the same three things, in the same order, in the same words the OS will use. The DMG's job is no longer decoration — it is an instruction.
Frame 3 · A sense of place
"I live up there," with a dotted line drawn to the real menu-bar icon, which does a three-beat amplitude swell on first launch only. This is the fix for "the app vanished" — not a Dock icon, an address. Dock icon stays available in Advanced, off by default.
Frame 4 · Sequenced permissions
Rows reveal one at a time; the next Allow only appears once the previous grant lands. Each row carries a limit as well as a reason — "I don't read what's on your screen" does more for trust than any badge. The escape hatch sits under the card from the start, not after failure.
Frames 5–7 · Wait, then payoff
The download reports MB, rate and ETA — the three numbers that make waiting bearable — while Know-Me absorbs the time. Then one full-window moment: the sentence, the ring, and the only stat that matters. It is saved and resurfaces on Home forever.
03 · THE HUD
One line. It never breaks, it never leaves, it just changes what it's doing.

Today the HUD draws five unrelated paths and cuts between them. The redesign treats it as a single stroke that persists across every state — the wave settles into a coil, the coil unwinds into a line, light runs down the line, the line folds into a tick. Nothing appears, nothing disappears; the same object is doing a different job. That continuity is the signature, and it costs no new pixels.

290 → 172 pt at rest
The chip and the timer both leave. What remains is the waveform and nothing else — 172 × 38, about the footprint of a Spotlight result row. It grows only when it has words to say: 300 for a success echo, 330 for an error with an action.
Success shows the text, not a count
"42 words" is a receipt. The last five words of what actually landed — "…on Friday." — is proof, and it is how you catch a bad transcription without looking away from your cursor. Dwell 1.4 s, then fade.
Bottom-centre stays — on principle
Following the caret means reading every app's text position through Accessibility. That is the one thing we promised not to do. Bottom-centre costs no permission, never occludes the insertion point, and is in the same place every single time — which is what "glanceable" actually means.

06 · VOICE & COPY
Say what happened. Then stop.
The rule
Second person, present tense, contractions. Report the outcome, never the mechanism. No ellipses — if something is in progress, the animation already says so. If a sentence can lose a word, lose it.
When the app says "I"
Only when asking for something or admitting a limit. "So I can hear you." "macOS can't launch me itself." Never for reporting facts — "Inserted 42 words" has no I in it, and shouldn't. This is why the welcome screen can say "I live up there" without being twee.
Never
No "we". No exclamation marks. No jokes the user has to read twice ("Hello from my own two vocal cords"). No model names, hostnames, checksums, or file paths outside a Details disclosure. No apology — say what to do next instead.

Nothing loops forever
Every animation in the app is either driven by real input (the waveform is your voice) or bounded by a real operation (the coil stops when transcription stops). There is no decorative idle animation anywhere, which is why the ring is allowed to breathe: it's the one exception, at 0.7 rad/s, and it only appears on surfaces you opened deliberately.
Frame rate is a state, not a constant
The HUD canvas runs at display rate while recording (it's tracking your voice), 20 fps while working (the coil reads identically), and stops entirely otherwise. The menu-bar animator already does this — extend the same discipline to TimelineView(.animation), which currently never idles.
VoiceOver runs parallel, not after
Existing labels are good. Two additions: the success state should announce the tail text, not the word count ("Inserted: …on Friday"), and the payoff moment needs an .accessibilityAddTraits(.isHeader) + posted announcement, or the single most important instant in the app is silent.
